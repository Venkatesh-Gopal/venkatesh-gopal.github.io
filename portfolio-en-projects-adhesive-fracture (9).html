STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY / COMPUTATIONAL FRACTURE

Fracture Modelling & Failure Assessment

Assessing crack initiation, propagation and material response with Abaqus XFEM, using reinforced adhesive specimens as the case study.

Analysis focus

Compare predicted crack paths and tensile responses with experiments across four fibre orientations. Examine where the directional material description captures the behaviour and where it needs improvement.

  • Abaqus
  • XFEM
  • Fracture mechanics
  • Material behaviour

Study at a glance

Item Scope
Material Epoxy adhesive containing short glass fibres.
Model Notched material specimen analysed with 3D Abaqus XFEM.
Orientations 0°, 30°, 60° and 90°.
Comparisons Crack paths and tensile stress–strain response.

Main findings

Comparison Finding
Crack paths Qualitative correspondence at 0° and 90°; substantial discrepancies at 30° and 60°.
Tensile response Predicted strains differed substantially from the experimental measurements.

Engineering interpretation

The simplified directional stiffness description was a priority for improvement. The comparison shows why a plausible crack path alone is insufficient to establish the model’s predictive quality.

Results refer to the published specimen study, which credits J. Philipp with the XFEM simulation. The model represents adhesive material, rather than a complete bonded blade.
