MASTER’S THESIS / BSH DISHWASHER PACKAGING

Honeycomb paperboard: testing and simulation

Material testing, LS-DYNA compression modelling and parameter calibration in LS-OPT for BSH dishwasher packaging.

The results shown here concern paper specimens and honeycomb board under compression. Dishwasher transport packaging provides the application context.

Application
BSH dishwasher packaging
Test specimen
Honeycomb board, 100 × 100 × 45 mm
Software
LS-DYNA, LS-OPT and LS-PrePost

Project overview

For my master’s thesis at BSH, I investigated the mechanical behaviour of honeycomb paperboard for dishwasher packaging. I characterised the liner and core paper, compared two LS-DYNA material models and assessed the response of the assembled board under compression.

  • LS-DYNA
  • MAT 58 / MAT 274
  • LS-OPT
  • Surrogate modelling

The engineering question

How well do material models calibrated at paper level reproduce the compression response of a honeycomb board?

ProblemMy workExperimentsMaterial modelsResultsLS-OPTConclusions
01

Engineering context

Honeycomb paperboard was investigated as a packaging material in the context of alternatives to expanded polystyrene. Numerical assessment required a representation of both the directional paper response and the crushing behaviour of the cellular board.

The machine direction (MD) and cross direction (CD) of paper differ in stiffness and strength. At board level, cell-wall deformation introduces a force peak, a crushing plateau and densification. The material model therefore needed assessment at both scales.

02

My contribution

  • Performed tensile and compression tests on liner and core paper in the machine and cross directions, together with out-of-plane compression tests on honeycomb board.
  • Developed and checked MAT 58 and MAT 274 material cards using experimental curves and single-element simulations.
  • Built honeycomb board compression models and compared their force–displacement curves with experiments.
  • Used metamodel-based parameter calibration in LS-OPT to address the mismatch obtained with MAT 58.

04

Material-level checks before board simulation

I compared MAT 58 (Laminated Composite Fabric) with MAT 274 (Paper). Single-element simulations checked the material response against the paper tests before the formulations were assessed in the honeycomb board model.

Formulation Paper-level comparison Board-level question
MAT 58 Tensile responses generally followed the measurements closely. Compression curves differed in slope and shape. Does the formulation reproduce the force level as the cells crush?
MAT 274 Tensile trends were generally close to the measurements. Compression reached similar stress levels with differences in the preceding response. Does the paper-specific formulation improve the structural comparison?
05

Board compression results

The experimental force–displacement response showed an initial peak, a lower crushing plateau and a steep rise during densification. Comparing these regions separately made the differences between the two formulations clear.

MAT 58 underpredicted the crushing force

Observation
The simulation captured the broad sequence of peak, crushing and densification, but substantially underpredicted the initial peak and much of the plateau force.
Interpretation
The expected stages were present, while the force level showed that the material and structural representation required improvement.
Engineering implication
Curve shape alone was insufficient for assessing cushioning performance. Force levels and absorbed energy require separate checks.

Source: thesis presentation, slide 25, Figure 28.

MAT 274 gave a closer plateau response

Observation
The initial peak and much of the plateau were closer to the experiment than with MAT 58. The simulation fell below the measured response after the peak and entered the final force rise earlier.
Interpretation
The paper-specific formulation improved the comparison for this specimen. Differences remained in the transitions into crushing and densification.
Engineering implication
MAT 274 was the stronger starting point for this compression case. Other geometries, loading rates and environmental conditions require further assessment.

Source: thesis presentation, slide 26, Figure 30.

06

Parameter calibration with LS-OPT

The mismatch obtained with MAT 58 motivated inverse identification of its material parameters. The calibration varied parameters in the material card to reduce the difference between the computed and experimental response.

  1. Parameter bounds

    Define admissible lower and upper bounds for the selected MAT 58 parameters.

  2. D-optimal sampling

    Select parameter combinations within those bounds for LS-DYNA evaluations.

  3. Simulation evaluations

    Run the LS-DYNA model at the selected combinations and extract its response.

  4. Surrogate construction

    Build a metamodel that approximates the simulated response as a function of the selected parameters. Use this approximation to support the search within the sampled region.

  5. Curve matching

    Use Curve Mapping to measure the mismatch between computed and experimental curves and guide parameter selection.

  6. SRSM refinement

    The described response-surface workflow adapts the search region through domain reduction when further refinement is required.

The surrogate approximates simulation evaluations. Curve Mapping measures agreement with an experimental target. Independent comparisons with additional solver evaluations are needed to assess the surrogate’s prediction accuracy.

Section 5.5 documents the calibration method. The supplied excerpt does not contain the final parameter values, convergence history or quantified improvement, so no percentage gain or runtime reduction is reported here.

07

Conclusions and scope

  • The study connected directional paper characterisation with the compression response of a cellular board.
  • The comparison favoured MAT 274 over MAT 58 for the investigated specimen and loading case.
  • Material-level agreement provided a useful check, while the board comparison exposed differences that the single-element checks did not resolve.
  • LS-OPT provided a systematic calibration approach for MAT 58. A separate before-and-after solver comparison is needed to establish its final benefit.

The evidence presented concerns specimen compression. It does not establish the drop performance of a fully packaged dishwasher, transport qualification or a measured environmental benefit. Humidity and air inside the cells were identified as subjects for further investigation.

Why it matters

The board-level comparison favoured MAT 274 for the investigated compression case. It also showed why agreement with paper tests alone does not establish a reliable prediction of the board’s crushing response.
