MASTER’S THESIS / BSH DISHWASHER PACKAGING

Material Characterisation & Nonlinear Simulation

Combining physical testing, finite element modelling and surrogate-based parameter calibration to investigate nonlinear material behaviour, demonstrated through a honeycomb paperboard study.

My contribution

I characterised liner and core paper, developed MAT 58 and MAT 274 material cards, and compared single-element and honeycomb board simulations with experiments. I also applied surrogate-based inverse modelling to calibrate MAT 58 parameters.

  • LS-DYNA
  • MAT 58 / MAT 274
  • LS-OPT
  • Surrogate modelling
01

Study at a glance

Item Scope
Application Paper-based protective packaging for dishwashers.
Experiments Liner and core paper in MD/CD tension and compression; board compression normal to its plane.
Board specimen 100 × 100 × 45 mm.
Simulation LS-DYNA: MAT 58 (Laminated Composite Fabric) and MAT 274 (Paper).
Calibration LS-OPT: D-optimal sampling, metamodels, Curve Mapping and SRSM refinement.
02

Main findings

Model Comparison with experiments
MAT 58 Captured the broad compression stages but substantially underpredicted the initial peak and much of the plateau force.
MAT 274 Gave a closer initial peak and plateau response. Differences remained after the peak and during the transition to densification.

MAT 274 was the stronger starting point for the investigated compression case. Material-level agreement alone did not establish the board’s crushing response.

03

Surrogate-based parameter calibration

To address the MAT 58 mismatch, I used bounded material parameters and D-optimal sampling to define LS-DYNA evaluations. LS-OPT built metamodels from those results and used Curve Mapping to assess agreement with the experimental target. The described SRSM workflow refined the search region through domain reduction.

The supplied material documents the method, without a final parameter set or quantified optimisation improvement.
