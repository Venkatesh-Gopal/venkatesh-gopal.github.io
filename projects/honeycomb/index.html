LIGHTWEIGHT STRUCTURES

Small cells. Complex behaviour.

Investigating honeycomb paperboard through material characterisation and numerical modelling.

The engineering question

How can a lightweight paper-based material be represented in a numerical model to investigate its response under load?

Project overview

Honeycomb paperboard has a cellular structure and a direction-dependent, nonlinear mechanical response. My work included tensile and compression testing, material-model calibration, and comparison of simulated and measured responses, as part of a broader investigation into packaging under transport, drop, and impact loading.

  • LS-DYNA
  • Material calibration
  • Honeycomb paperboard
  • Validation

My contribution

  • Characterised paper and honeycomb paperboard through tensile and compression tests.
  • Calibrated LS-DYNA material models using single-element and honeycomb models.
  • Prepared finite element models and evaluated their response against experimental data.
  • Investigated dishwasher and packaging configurations under transport, drop, and impact loading.

Technical focus

  • Start with physical tests to establish the behaviour the model needs to represent.
  • Examine the influence of material parameters through numerical studies.
  • Compare simulated responses with measurements and investigate differences.
  • Use the modelling work to support evaluation of packaging configurations.

Why it matters

A packaging model depends on how well it represents the material inside it. Connecting material characterisation with structural simulation makes predictions more useful for engineering evaluation.
